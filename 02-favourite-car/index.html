Car

Cars, also known as automobiles, are four-wheeled motor vehicles designed primarily for passenger transportation on roads. Ever since German inventor Karl Benz patented the Benz Patent-Motorwagen in 1886, cars have revolutionized how humanity moves, replacing horse-drawn carriages and providing unprecedented personal mobility, convenience, and independence. Structurally, they rely on complex engineering systems—including the engine or motor, transmission, battery, and brakes—to safely convert raw fuel or electrical energy into smooth movement.

  2. Supra MK4

    The Toyota Supra MK4 (A80) is a legendary Japanese sports car produced from 1993 to 2002 that has achieved iconic status in global automotive culture [Supra]. Best known for its ultra-robust, twin-turbocharged 3.0-litre 2JZ-GTE inline-six engine, the car left the factory with highly capable performance but gained its true fame in the aftermarket tuning scene, where its engine block could reliably handle over 1,000 horsepower with stock internals. Complemented by a timeless, flowing 1990s aerodynamic design, a driver-centric cockpit layout reminiscent of a fighter jet, and a starring role in pop culture—most notably as Brian O'Conner's signature car in the Fast & Furious franchise—the MK4 transformed from an underappreciated sports coupe of its era into a highly coveted modern classic.

  3. BMW M4

    The BMW M4 is a high-performance sports coupe that combines aggressive design, luxury, and track-ready capabilities. BMW India +2 At the heart of the M4 lies a powerful 3.0-liter twin-turbocharged inline-six engine. In top-tier variants like the Competition with M xDrive, this engine pumps out 530 horsepower and 650 Nm of torque. Paired with an 8-speed M Steptronic transmission and an all-wheel-drive system, it can rocket from 0 to 100 km/h in just 3.5 seconds. Renowned for its razor-sharp handling, carbon-fiber exterior elements, and driver-focused cockpit, the M4 serves as the iconic two-door standard for modern high-speed engineering

  4. Nissan GTR

    The Nissan GT-R, famously nicknamed "Godzilla," is a high-performance sports car built by Nissan from 2007 to 2025. Wikipedia +1 Powered by a handcrafted 3.8-liter twin-turbocharged V6 engine and an advanced all-wheel-drive system, the GT-R delivers jaw-dropping acceleration, going from 0 to 100 km/h in roughly 3 seconds. It earned a legendary reputation worldwide for matching or beating expensive European supercars at a fraction of the cost. Production of the iconic R35 generation officially ended in 2025, leaving behind a massive legacy in automotive and motorsport history.
